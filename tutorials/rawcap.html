What Is RAW?

RAW is an image format that keeps much more of the original camera sensor data than JPEG. A RAW photo is saved with very little processing, so the image contains more detail for editing later.

RAW photos usually preserve:

  • More color information
  • Greater dynamic range
  • Better shadow and highlight detail
  • More flexibility during editing
  • Less compression

This is why RAW is so popular in serious photography and why a mobile RAW photography guide matters for users who want proper editing control instead of relying only on automatic camera processing.

In simple words, RAW keeps the file closer to what the camera sensor actually captured. That gives you more room to fix mistakes later, especially when lighting is bad or the shot is slightly underexposed.

What Is DNG?

DNG, short for Digital Negative, is a RAW image format developed by Adobe.

Instead of every phone maker using a different RAW format, DNG gives you a more universal file type that works with many editing apps and desktop tools.

A DNG file contains much more image information than a standard JPEG, which makes it easier to recover details, adjust colors, and improve exposure during editing. That is the core idea behind a good DNG editing workflow: capture more data first, edit later with more control.

Popular apps that support DNG include:

  • Adobe Lightroom
  • Snapseed
  • Darktable
  • RawTherapee

If your goal is mobile editing, DNG is one of the best file formats to work with because it keeps more of the original image data intact before any heavy compression or processing happens.

What Is Camera2 API?

Camera2 API is Google’s modern camera framework for Android. It allows camera apps to communicate more directly with the camera hardware and unlock features that the stock app may hide.

Depending on the device, Camera2 API can provide:

  • RAW (DNG) capture
  • Manual focus
  • Manual ISO control
  • Manual shutter speed
  • White balance adjustment
  • Exposure compensation
  • Better support for third-party camera apps

Since the Redmi A3 can work with Camera2 API, Open Camera can use these features right away.

This is exactly why Camera2 API matters for people searching for Android Camera2 API tutorial content: it is the bridge between basic mobile photography and more advanced control.

What Is Open Camera?

Open Camera is a free and open-source camera app for Android.

Unlike many stock camera apps, Open Camera offers manual controls and advanced options that are usually found on more expensive phones. It is lightweight, regularly updated, and widely trusted by Android users.

For the Redmi A3, Open Camera is useful because it can access Camera2 features, expose RAW capture, and give you more control over how the camera behaves during shooting.

Why Shoot in RAW Instead of JPEG?

JPEG photos are processed automatically before being saved. During that process, some image data is discarded to make the file smaller.

RAW files keep much more of the original sensor data, which helps you:

  • Recover blown highlights
  • Brighten dark shadows
  • Adjust white balance more safely
  • Reduce noise more effectively
  • Apply proper color grading

If you enjoy editing photos, RAW usually gives noticeably better results than JPEG.

This is the main reason people move from quick camera snapshots to a proper DNG editing workflow: JPEG is faster and smaller, but RAW gives you much more freedom during post-processing.

Step-by-Step Guide: Enabling Camera2 API on Xiaomi Redmi A3

Step 1: Install Open Camera

[Screenshot of opencamera in playstore, filename: opcam-install.webp]

  1. Open the Google Play Store.
  2. Search for Open Camera
  3. Download and install the app.

This app is the foundation of the entire process. Without it, the stock camera may not expose the controls needed for RAW capture.

Step 2: Grant Required Permissions

  1. Open "Open Camera"

    [screenshot of permissions menu and clicking allow on them, filename: opcam-permit.webp]

  2. Allow all requested permissions.
  3. Tap the Settings icon.

    [Screenshot of clicking on settings button in openCamera, filename: opcam-clickset.webp]

The app needs camera permissions and related access to function properly. If any permission is denied, certain options may not appear or may fail to work correctly.

Step 3: Enable Camera2 API

  1. Open Settings.

    [Settings screenshot, opcam-set.webp]

  2. Tap Camera API.

    [Camera API selection menu screenshot, opcam-setapi.webp]

  3. Select Camera2 API.

    [Scrshot of selecting camera2API, opcam-set2api.webp]

  4. Wait for the app to restart automatically.

Once Camera2 API is selected, Open Camera can communicate with the Redmi A3 camera hardware more directly. This is the key step for enabling advanced features and RAW capture.

Step 4: Enable RAW (DNG) Capture

  1. Open Settings again.

    [Screenshot of settings page, opcam-set.webp]

  2. Go to Photo Settings.

    [Photo settings scrshot, opcam-photoset.webp]

  3. Tap RAW.

    [Clicking on RAW scrshot, opcam-raw.webp]

  4. Choose DNG RAW only.

    [Selecting DNG(RAW Only) scrshot, opcam-dngselect.webp]

  5. Confirm by tapping OK.

That is it. RAW capture is now enabled.

[Scrshot proving raw is enabled, raw-proof.webp]

At this point, your Redmi A3 is ready to save RAW files directly from the camera app, which is exactly what this Redmi A3 camera optimization method is designed to do.

Where Are RAW Photos Saved?

Open Camera usually saves RAW photos as .DNG files.

In most cases, they are stored in the DCIM/OpenCamera folder in internal storage, depending on your settings.

You can open these files in apps like Adobe Lightroom or Snapseed, or transfer them to a computer for deeper editing.

Why this matters

If you are building a proper DNG editing workflow, knowing where the files are stored is important because it makes importing, sorting, and editing much faster. RAW files can be larger, so they may also be easier to manage if you move them into a dedicated folder after capture.

Editing RAW Photos on Android

RAW files are not meant to look perfect immediately. They are meant to be edited. For the full workflow, see: Editing RAW Photos on Android.

That workflow usually includes:

  1. Importing the DNG file

This is where RAW becomes powerful. Instead of relying on automatic processing, you decide how the final image should look. That is the core benefit of mobile RAW photography guide style shooting.

Things to Keep in Mind

Before using RAW mode, remember:

  • RAW files are much larger than JPEGs.
  • RAW photos usually need editing to look their best.
  • Saving RAW files may take a little longer.
  • RAW does not magically improve every photo; it preserves more data for later editing.

This is important because many users expect RAW to instantly look better than JPEG. That is not how it works. RAW gives you more editing potential, but the final quality still depends on how you process the photo.

Troubleshooting

If something does not work properly, use this section first.

I do not see the RAW option

Make sure you have:

  • Selected Camera2 API inside Open Camera
  • Restarted the app after changing the camera API
  • Updated Open Camera to the latest version

If the option still does not appear, your device or current software version may not fully expose RAW support to third-party apps.

The DNG files look darker than JPEGs

That is normal. RAW files are minimally processed and are meant to be edited before sharing.

RAW photos are taking too long to save

This usually happens because RAW files are bigger and require more writing time than compressed JPEG files. It is not a bug; it is just the cost of preserving more sensor data.

My storage is filling up quickly

That is expected too. Since DNG files are larger, you should keep an eye on storage if you use RAW frequently. This is another reason why post-processing and regular file cleanup matter.
